Changelog

What's been built and why. Most recent first.

Editorial redesign 1 May 2026 (evening)

Visual overhaul. Every page now shares a single stylesheet (docs/style.css) and a wire-service masthead — Playfair Display headlines, Public Sans body, JetBrains Mono for metadata, deep editorial red as the signature accent and a paper-tone background. The homepage is now the most recent daily brief inline, with the archive listed below and the comparison pages featured in their own panel. Tier signalling moved from coloured pills to small coloured squares in the source-line, which is quieter on the page. Section breaks use a small ❦ ornament to mark transitions.

Mobile: tap targets bumped to 40px+, type ladder reduces sensibly, filter pills wrap. Accessibility: skip link, focus rings, semantic landmarks, no relying on colour alone for tier signalling (the source-line text already carries the source family).

Three ISW comparisons (April 21, 24, 30) 1 May 2026 (afternoon)

Two more like-for-like comparisons against ISW Iran Update reports. Each maps ISW's themes to our coverage with green/amber/red badges, and calls out items we caught that ISW didn't.

21 April: 19 matched items. The State Department's Mahan Air sanctions designation lands the same day, directly matching ISW's proliferation-networks theme. UK Parliament API was returning 500s during the run so any tabled questions on 20–21 April are missing — diagnostics record the failure honestly.

24 April: 22 matched items, the strongest UK case of the three. We catch the Vahidi-Ghalibaf delegation breakdown via Iran International, the State Dept China-oil sanctions on the day, the OFAC Federal Register notice, and a substantial UK political layer ISW doesn't touch — Starmer's IRGC-ban pledge, the LBC sabotage investigation, the Old Bailey spying case, and five Parliament Q&As on Arms Trade.

April 19–30 daily archive backfilled 1 May 2026

Twelve days of dated briefs, each with a 24-hour calendar-day window. Total: 89 events across the period, range from 2 items (19 April — feed-history floor) to 13 items (21 April — IRGC firing on Indian vessel, US-led blockade, Tehran moderates piece). Earlier April days aren't backfilled because the RSS feeds underneath most of our sources only retain ~10–150 recent items and don't reach further back.

Each daily page got prev/next navigation top and bottom, so the editor can step through the chain without bouncing back to the index.

Comparison vs. ISW Iran Update — 30 April 2026 1 May 2026

Reran the collector against the same calendar window as the ISW Iran Update Special Report dated 30 April 2026 — added an --until argument so we can reconstruct historical 24-or-48-hour windows. 48 hours ending end-of-day 30 April produced 18 items, against ISW's roughly 3,000-word synthesis covering the same horizon. The comparison page lays our 18 items against ISW's themes, theme by theme.

Verdict: we caught the geopolitical core (Strait of Hormuz, US-led maritime coalition, internal Iran politics, Iran's economic decline), partially caught the regime-domestic-life angle (more strongly than ISW does, because Iran International is leaning into it), and went silent on the tactical military layer — Khamenei's direct quotes via Telegram, Hezbollah's drone strikes, IDF strike counts on Lebanon, Israeli casualties. All three of those silences are Telegram and X gaps that the v1 scope already flagged. Nothing surprising, but useful to be precise about.

Daily window, filter pills, rich Parliament layout 1 May 2026

Three changes on the back of an editorial review. (1) The brief is now actually a brief — we were running with a 14-day backfill window when we should have been running 24 hours. (2) Filter pills along the top group items by source family with live counts and toggle individual sources on/off. (3) Parliament events render with a proper Q&A layout: asking member with party tag and house, the department asked, the date tabled, the full question text, and — when the answer has landed — the answering minister with party, the date, and the full answer.

Iran International added; double-escape fix 1 May 2026

Wired in Iran International as a tier-3 source — the opposition outlet's English RSS feed is rich and well-tagged, and with Iran's domestic internet still in blackout it's the closest thing to an inside-Iran view we can pull cleanly. The brief jumped from 38 items to 126 over the same 14-day window, with the new 88 covering the politics-of-pink propaganda piece, the IRGC firing on an Indian vessel in Hormuz, the dispute within the leadership that blocked the negotiators' trip to Islamabad, the Mashhad protest crackdown. None of which were going to land via FCDO written answers.

Tier-3 badge is the right home for it — the editorial slant is real and worth signalling. A future job is a parallel triangulation feed (Tasnim or IRNA via mirror, or BBC Monitoring if we ever pay for it) so the brief presents a left-and-right-of-Tehran read rather than a left-only one.

Tried and dropped from this round: the IDF press page (idf.il) is a JavaScript SPA that returns a 212-byte shell — needs Playwright or a headless browser, which is a chunk of work and a chunk of dependencies for one source. Deferred to v2.

v1 prototype 1 May 2026

First end-to-end run. Six source adapters wired up, filter and de-dup working, daily HTML page rendering. The morning brief for 1 May has 38 items across UK Parliament, Federal Register / OFAC, US State Department, UN Press, IAEA and the UK FCDO / No 10 / MoD via gov.uk's Atom feeds.

Honest gaps from day one: ReliefWeb is wired but waits on an appname registration that ReliefWeb tightened in November 2025. The US Department of War (the rebranded DoD) and CENTCOM are 403-blocked at the WAF level — most of what we'd want from them is duplicated through State and the Federal Register, but the gap is real. Telegram, X and Iranian state media are firmly out of scope for v1.

The biggest surprise on the first run was how much UK Parliament was already pulling its weight: the MoD was being asked about the origin of the drone that struck RAF Akrotiri, the Home Office about British nationals leaving the UAE, the FCDO about UN committee nominations. That's exactly the kind of national-record-of-record material a UK tip-sheet should be surfacing, and most of it never makes the wires.
