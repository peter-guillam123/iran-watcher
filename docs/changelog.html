Daily window, filter pills, rich Parliament layout 1 May 2026 (later)

Three changes on the back of Chris's review. (1) The brief is now actually a brief — we were running with a 14-day backfill window when we should have been running 24 hours. The page header now reads "window 30 Apr 08:14Z to 01 May 08:14Z" and on a quiet day like today's that's six items. On a busy day it'll be a couple of dozen. The first run after Parliament returns from prorogation will tell us properly.

(2) Filter pills along the top group items by source family with live counts ("UK Parliament 3 · US State Department 5 · Iran International 45") and toggle individual sources on/off. Click two and you get the union — "show me Parliament and State Department". Click "All" to clear. Vanilla JS, no framework.

(3) Parliament events now render with a proper Q&A layout: asking member with party tag and house, the department asked, the date tabled, the full question text, and — when the answer has landed — the answering minister with party, the date, and the full answer. Lord Alton (Crossbench, Lords) → FCDO, Ruth Jones (Labour, Commons) → FCDO on the Bahá'í community, Lee Barron (Labour, Commons) → Home Office on UAE returnees. The kind of richness that makes the items actually scannable instead of opaque.

Also: cleaned the "Thomas Pigott, Department Spokesperson, Press Statement [date]" boilerplate that the State Department RSS prepends to every body, so the summary actually starts with the news.

The list at docs/index.html currently shows two pages: today's daily 24-hour brief, and a one-time "last 7 days demo" so anyone who wants to see what the layout does at scale (54 items, all sources represented) has somewhere to look. The demo file goes away once the daily cron has run a few times.

Iran International added; double-escape fix 1 May 2026 (afternoon)

Wired in Iran International as a tier-3 source — the opposition outlet's English RSS feed is rich and well-tagged, and with Iran's domestic internet still in blackout it's the closest thing to an inside-Iran view we can pull cleanly. The brief jumped from 38 items to 126 over the same 14-day window, with the new 88 covering the politics-of-pink propaganda piece, the IRGC firing on an Indian vessel in Hormuz, the dispute within the leadership that blocked the negotiators' trip to Islamabad, the Mashhad protest crackdown. None of which were going to land via FCDO written answers.

Tier-3 badge is the right home for it — the editorial slant is real and worth signalling. A future job is a parallel triangulation feed (Tasnim or IRNA via mirror, or BBC Monitoring if we ever pay for it) so the brief presents a left-and-right-of-Tehran read rather than a left-only one.

Also: small render fix — State Department summaries arrive with HTML entities pre-encoded (&quot;, &#8217;), and we were re-escaping them. html.unescape first, then re-escape, so quotes and apostrophes render properly.

Tried and dropped from this round: the IDF press page (idf.il) is a JavaScript SPA that returns a 212-byte shell — needs Playwright or a headless browser, which is a chunk of work and a chunk of dependencies for one source. Deferred to v2.

v1 prototype 1 May 2026

First end-to-end run. Six source adapters wired up, filter and de-dup working, daily HTML page rendering. The morning brief for 1 May has 38 items across UK Parliament, Federal Register / OFAC, US State Department, UN Press, IAEA and the UK FCDO / No 10 / MoD via gov.uk's Atom feeds.

Honest gaps from day one: ReliefWeb is wired but waits on an appname registration that ReliefWeb tightened in November 2025. The US Department of War (the rebranded DoD) and CENTCOM are 403-blocked at the WAF level — most of what we'd want from them is duplicated through State and the Federal Register, but the gap is real. Telegram, X and Iranian state media are firmly out of scope for v1, with the Iran blackout being the strongest argument that the last of those isn't really available right now anyway.

The biggest surprise on the first run was how much UK Parliament was already pulling its weight: the MoD was being asked about the origin of the drone that struck RAF Akrotiri, the Home Office about British nationals leaving the UAE, the FCDO about UN committee nominations. That's exactly the kind of national-record-of-record material a UK tip-sheet should be surfacing, and most of it never makes the wires.
