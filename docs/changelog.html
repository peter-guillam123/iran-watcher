Brief presentation pass — five changes for scale 3 May 2026

The brief is going to get noisier. Bluesky added 20 items today, OCHA / ReliefWeb is one appname registration away, UK Parliament will go from zero to ~30 items a day when it's back from recess. So a presentation pass before the volume lands.

Five changes, in priority order. (1) "Today's shape" — a single one-sentence kicker at the very top of every brief, generated from the same Opus call that produces the threads. Names the most consequential developments concretely, with figures and named bodies. The 5-second skim a 7am editor reads before scrolling. (2) Telegram regime-channel clusters — the 35-items-a-day Khamenei + IRIB + Defa Press layer collapses into one block per channel, with the latest item as a teaser and an expander for the rest. Inline-chronological they were swamping the primary-source layer; now they sit in their own zone at the bottom of the brief with a thin accent rule and a "REGIME-CHANNEL LAYER" caption. (3) "fig." marker — small monospace badge on cards that contain specific verifiable figures (40+ sites, 45 vessels, $344m, 27%). Detection is regex-based, runs after the translation pass so it catches the English version of Persian / Arabic posts, and is paired with a "With figures only" filter toggle. The "writeable from" cards in one click. (4) "Primary sources only" toggle — hides tiers 3 + 4 (Iran International + regime channels) so the reader can see what governments and multilateral bodies are saying directly. Single button replacing five-pill-deselects. (5) Clickable threads — each thread already cites the events it clusters; clicking the thread title now filters the events list below to just those items. The topical-filter mechanism without inventing a flat topic taxonomy.

Five filter axes now live and orthogonal: source family, sort (date/tier), primary-only, figures-only, current-thread. Each answers a distinct question. No two slice the same data the same way. The synthesis pass also auto-bumps from a 5-thread to a 7-thread cap on days with more than 60 items, since 5 themes is too coarse a partition over a sitting-day brief.

Times of Israel and Jerusalem Post via Bluesky 3 May 2026

Both English-language Israeli newspapers run active Bluesky accounts (timesofisrael.com and thejerusalempost.bsky.social) that auto-post their headlines with an external embed pointing to the article URL. The AT Protocol exposes a fully public, unauthenticated read API — no key, no free-tier slot, no third-party scraper. sources/bluesky.py pulls the latest 100 posts per account, extracts the article URL from each post's external embed (or falls back to the Bluesky permalink for image-only posts like quote cards), and emits at tier 2 alongside State Department and gov.uk Atom.

Smoke test on a 2-day window: 161 posts fetched across both accounts, 41 kept after the local Iran filter — the right kind of cull. The kept items are precisely the headlines a UK defence correspondent wants surfaced (Trump's "renew strikes if they misbehave", Iranian officer warning of likely war, Hezbollah rocket fire, the Spirit Airlines bankruptcy "amid Iran war price fuel surge").

The honest limitation: this closes the English Israeli press gap, not the Hebrew one. ISW's tactical-operational lift comes from Mako, YNet (in Hebrew), Channel 12 and KAN — none of which are meaningfully on Bluesky. The named Hebrew-press reporters whose scoops ISW cites (Manni Fabian, Barak Ravid, Yossi Melman) all created BS accounts in 2024 and effectively stopped posting; they're back on X now. The Hebrew layer stays in the v2 pile, achievable via polite scrape with translation, and the About page is honest about it.

IDF and CENTCOM via X (rss.app) 2 May 2026

Closes most of the tactical-military gap to ISW. Chris set up two free rss.app feeds wrapping the IDF and US Central Command X accounts, and a new sources/x_via_rssapp.py adapter pulls them as JSON Feed. From the live feeds at the moment of the first fetch: IDF saying "40+ Hezbollah infrastructure sites dismantled" (verbatim the ISW headline number for 1 May), "7,500+ weapons in southern Lebanon" and individual strike confirmations; CENTCOM saying "45 commercial vessels redirected" (also ISW's verbatim figure) plus USS New Orleans patrol confirmations.

Both are surfaced at tier 2 — same provenance band as State Department or gov.uk Atom — but the source-line on each card reads "via rss.app" so the third-party-scraping delivery mechanism is visible. The threads system prompt was already strict about phrasing claims as claims, which catches IDF/CENTCOM operational claims as well as it catches regime-channel ones.

One bug shipped en route: the local keyword filter dropped IDF posts that said "southern Lebanon" without naming Hezbollah, since "lebanon" wasn't in the IRAN_TERMS list. Fixed by trusting the curated feed (needs_local_filter=False) — these are official military-spox broadcasts during a live Iran/Hezbollah war, on-beat by definition.

Cron shifted to 07:00 UK time 2 May 2026

Was 06:00 + 18:00 UTC twice a day. Now once a day at 06:00 UTC, which lands at 07:00 BST through UK summer. In UK winter the brief arrives at 06:00 UK, an hour earlier than the nominal 07:00 — GitHub cron doesn't follow DST. Early is fine for an editorial product.

Five threads + translation pipeline 1 May 2026 (evening)

Two Claude API passes added on top of the collection pipeline. The first translates Persian, Arabic and Hebrew Telegram items via claude-haiku-4-5 with a structured-output schema, cached on disk by event id so re-runs are free. The second is a single claude-opus-4-7 call per run that clusters the day's events into up to five themed two-sentence blurbs.

The synthesis system prompt is strict about NOT editorialising — no "this suggests", no "amid", no "raising concerns", no "experts say". Each thread cites at least two event ids; threads that draw on tier-4 (regime-channel) or military-spox claims get an "includes claims" badge so a Khamenei broadcast in a thread is visibly flagged. Combined cost: roughly £0.04/run.

A backfill workflow retro-fits both passes onto historical archive days without re-fetching events from sources, since most of the underlying RSS feeds don't reach back far enough to make re-fetching meaningful.

Telegram regime-channel adapter 1 May 2026 (afternoon)

Three Iranian regime channels via the public t.me/s/ web preview: Khamenei's office (rahbar_enghelab_ir), IRIB state broadcaster (iribnews), and the IRGC-affiliated Defa Press (defapress_ir). No Telegram account needed; ~80 lines of Python with bs4. Surfaces at tier 4 with a deep-red 2px left rule, smaller headline type, and an "includes claims" badge wherever a thread relies on these items.

What didn't work via this scraping path: the IDF Telegram, IDF Farsi, Hezbollah's mmirleb, and Tasnim — those have the public preview disabled or 500-fail on rss-bridge. Reaching them needs MTProto via Telethon, which requires a phone number and a Guardian institutional sign-off conversation.

Editorial redesign 1 May 2026 (afternoon)

Visual overhaul applied from a Claude Design handoff. Every page now shares a single stylesheet (docs/style.css), a wire-service masthead with a 4px black top rule and a 96px deep-red ribbon, real fonts (Playfair Display headlines, Public Sans body, JetBrains Mono for metadata), a paper-tone background, and a watch-dial / radar-sweep mark beside the wordmark. Section breaks use a small ❦ ornament. prefers-reduced-motion is respected throughout.

Page structure also tidied: the homepage is now today's brief inline ("Today's report") with a live-updating "last updated X ago" timestamp. Archive moved to its own /archive.html page; Comparisons got its own nav category for evaluation artefacts. One canonical 768px width across every page so the design no longer jumps when you click between sections. The mobile nav becomes a single-row horizontal scroll strip below 640px.

Four ISW comparisons 1 May 2026

Four like-for-like comparisons against ISW Iran Update reports — 21 April, 24 April, 30 April and 1 May. Each maps ISW's themes to our coverage with green/amber/red badges, and calls out items we caught that ISW didn't.

The 1 May comparison is the strongest of the set. We landed both same-day State Department oil-trade sanctions designations and we have the full Khamenei Workers' Day / Persian Gulf messaging cycle direct from rahbar_enghelab_ir — material ISW only references obliquely. The 24 April comparison is where the UK angle is loudest: Starmer's IRGC-ban pledge, the LBC sabotage investigation, the Old Bailey spying case, and five Parliament Q&As on Arms Trade.

Added an --until argument so we can reconstruct historical 24-or-48-hour windows for matched-window analysis. Each comparison is built from a 48h window ending end-of-day on the ISW report's date.

April 19–30 daily archive backfilled 1 May 2026

Twelve days of dated briefs. Total: 89 events across the period (later supplemented to 117 once Parliament data was successfully re-fetched after API timeouts). Each daily page got prev/next navigation top and bottom; once the threads pipeline shipped, the backfill workflow added a five-thread synthesis to every archive day too.

Earlier April days aren't backfilled because the RSS feeds underneath most of our sources only retain ~10–150 recent items and don't reach further back. Telegram preview is even shorter (~20 most recent posts per channel), so historical Telegram coverage isn't really possible — the regime-channel layer only joins from 1 May onwards.

Daily window, filter pills, rich Parliament layout 1 May 2026

Three changes on the back of an editorial review. (1) The brief is now a 24-hour rolling window — was a 14-day backfill. (2) Filter pills along the top group items by source family with live counts and toggle individual sources on/off; sort options for date or tier. (3) Parliament events render with a proper Q&A layout: asking member with party tag and house, the department asked, the date tabled, the full question text, and — when the answer has landed — the answering minister with party, the date, and the full answer.

Iran International added 1 May 2026

Wired in Iran International as a tier-3 source — the opposition outlet's English RSS feed is rich and well-tagged, and with Iran's domestic internet still in blackout it's the closest thing to an inside-Iran view we can pull cleanly. Tier-3 badge is the right home for it — the editorial slant is real and worth signalling.

Tried and dropped from this round: the IDF press page (idf.il) is a JavaScript SPA that returns a 212-byte shell. Eventually solved on 2 May via the rss.app X wrapper instead.

v1 prototype 1 May 2026

First end-to-end run. Six source adapters wired up, filter and de-dup working, daily HTML page rendering. The morning brief for 1 May has 38 items across UK Parliament, Federal Register / OFAC, US State Department, UN Press, IAEA and the UK FCDO / No 10 / MoD via gov.uk's Atom feeds.

Honest gaps from day one: ReliefWeb is wired but waits on an appname registration that ReliefWeb tightened in November 2025. The US Department of War (the rebranded DoD) and CENTCOM are 403-blocked at the WAF level — most of what we'd want from them is duplicated through State and the Federal Register, but the gap is real. Telegram, X and Iranian state media are firmly out of scope for v1.

The biggest surprise on the first run was how much UK Parliament was already pulling its weight: the MoD was being asked about the origin of the drone that struck RAF Akrotiri, the Home Office about British nationals leaving the UAE, the FCDO about UN committee nominations. That's exactly the kind of national-record-of-record material a UK tip-sheet should be surfacing, and most of it never makes the wires.
