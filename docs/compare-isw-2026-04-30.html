Iran Watcher vs. ISW

A like-for-like comparison: same calendar window (29–30 April 2026), reading our matched-window output against the ISW Iran Update Special Report dated 30 April 2026. Browse our matched-window output (18 items).

The ISW report is a daily 3,000-word synthesised brief written by six named analysts. About half its citations come from Telegram and X — both deliberately out of scope for our v1. The point of this exercise isn't to claim we match it (we don't), it's to be precise about what we catch and where we go silent, so the correspondent can see whether we'd be a complementary input or just a thin substitute.

What this tells us

Our matched-window output catches the geopolitical core that ISW reports on (Strait of Hormuz, US-led coalition, internal Iranian politics, Iran's economic decline) and adds a UK-government and Iranian-domestic-life layer ISW underweights. We are silent on the tactical military beat — Khamenei's direct quotes, Hezbollah's drone strikes, IDF strike counts, Israeli casualties — because all three rely on Telegram and X, both out of scope for v1.

For the correspondent: this is plausibly a complementary read alongside ISW rather than a substitute. ISW remains the only source for the tactical military layer; we add timely UK Parliament, OFAC sanctions notices the day they land, FCDO statements, and a denser inside-Iran view via Iran International. The Telegram gap is the single biggest lever — closing it would be the v2 question.

Caveats on this comparison: 18 items isn't a full day's tip-sheet; on a busy news day with Parliament sitting and OFAC active we'd expect 30–60. The 30 April calendar day was thin on UK Parliament because of Prorogation and on Federal Register because no Iran-specific notices published that day. A different sample day might shift the balance.
