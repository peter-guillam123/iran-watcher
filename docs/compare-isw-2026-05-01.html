What this tells us

The original 1 May read of this page argued that ISW's irreplaceable advantage was the tactical-military layer — strike counts, blockade numbers — coming from Telegram and X channels we couldn't reach. That argument is now half-true. With IDF and CENTCOM via X added on 2 May, both of the verbatim figures ISW leads on (40+ Hezbollah sites, 45 vessels turned around) appear in our feed within minutes of posting, from the same official broadcasters. The one tactical layer we still genuinely miss is the Hezbollah side — its mmirleb Telegram with the 12-attack tally and fibre-optic-drone detail — and the Israeli Hebrew press read on Iranian air-defence reactivation.

What we add on top of catching ISW's headlines: same-day State Department oil-trade sanctions designations, the full Khamenei sermon arc with English translations, three UK FCDO statutory-guidance documents on how UK businesses are expected to navigate the war, and the bottom-up Iran International coverage of the domestic economic collapse. None of this is in the ISW report.

So for 1 May specifically, the matched-window read goes from "complementary, with a real gap" to "matched on the headlines, complementary on the rest". A UK defence correspondent reading our brief now has ISW's two lead numbers without needing to open ISW. They'd still want ISW for the analytical synthesis (the framing, the rial chart, the geolocation) and for the Hezbollah-side tactical detail. But the urgent same-day signal is no longer a one-way trade.
