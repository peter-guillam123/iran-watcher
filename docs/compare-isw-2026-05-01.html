What this tells us

For 1 May specifically, the case for using both is sharpest. ISW gives the tactical military layer no one else has — IDF strike counts, Hezbollah's drone tally, CENTCOM's blockade numbers, the fibre-optic FPV-drone operational detail. We give same-day OFAC and State Department sanctions designations, the full Khamenei sermon arc with English translations, three UK FCDO documents tracking how UK businesses are expected to navigate the war, and a denser Iran-domestic read than ISW operates in.

The overlap on themes is roughly 50% (3 of 8 ISW themes caught directly). The non-overlap is roughly even-handed: ISW has tactical-military content we miss; we have UK-government and human-rights content ISW skips. A UK defence correspondent reading both gets the full picture. Reading either alone leaves a gap.

The biggest editorial signal of the day, by far, is that both outputs lead with the same theme: the US blockade is squeezing Iranian oil revenue and the Iranian economy is breaking. ISW has the macro chart (the rial line graph since January 2025); we have the bottom-up Iran International coverage (medicine prices, hotel evictions, layoffs, wage anxiety) plus the regime's own response in Khamenei's Workers' Day messaging. Two mostly-independent sourcing paths, the same conclusion.
