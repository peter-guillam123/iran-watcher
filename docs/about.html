About

A tip sheet, not a report. Built for a defence correspondent; left public so anyone can read.

This is a daily aggregator of primary governmental, multilateral and military-broadcast sources on Iran and the Middle East crisis. It pulls from public APIs, RSS feeds, scraped Telegram previews and an X-via-rss.app wrapper, filters for Iran-relevance, de-duplicates, runs a Persian/Arabic translation pass and a five-thread synthesis pass, and publishes a per-day page. The point is to surface the documentary record — what governments, multilateral bodies and military spokespeople are saying officially — fast, with permalinks, before the wires do their thing.

It is explicitly not trying to be the ISW Iran Update, but it now reaches a meaningful chunk of what ISW reaches. The ISW report is a synthesised analyst product written by six named researchers, and a substantial slice of its citations are Telegram channels and X posts. We now reach the regime-channel half of that (Khamenei's office, IRIB, Defa Press) directly, plus the IDF and CENTCOM X feeds via a third-party rss.app wrapper. What we still don't reach is Hezbollah's Telegram, Israeli press in Hebrew, and the OSINT analyst constellation on X. See Comparisons for like-for-like reads against four ISW reports.

What's in

  • UK Parliament — every written question and ministerial statement that mentions Iran. Tabled questions and answered questions emit separately, so you catch both the signal of an MP asking and the substance when a minister responds. Tier 1: official API.
  • US Federal Register — executive orders, proclamations, agency rules, and (crucially) OFAC sanctions notices. Filtered for "Iran". Tier 1: official API.
  • UN press releases — Security Council, General Assembly, Secretary-General. Global feed; filtered locally. Tier 2: RSS.
  • IAEA — the agency's news feed. Mostly noise from a Tehran-watching point of view (a lot of capacity-building in Vietnam etc.) but Iran-specific updates land here first. Tier 2: RSS.
  • US State Department — three feeds: press releases, the Near East regional feed, and Secretary's remarks. The Near East feed is the strongest signal; the press release feed catches the rest. Tier 2: RSS.
  • UK Government departments via gov.uk Atom — FCDO, MoD, No 10, HM Treasury, Cabinet Office. Department-wide feeds; filtered locally for Iran-relevance. Tier 2: Atom.
  • IDF and CENTCOM via X (rss.app wrapper) — Israel Defense Forces and US Central Command broadcast strike confirmations, blockade tallies, recovered-weapons counts, and operational claims via X. We pull both via rss.app, a third-party JSON Feed wrapper. The source-line on each card reads "via rss.app" so the third-party-scraping delivery mechanism is visible. Editorially these are official-body broadcasts and partial in the same way every press-spox is partial — IDF will say its strikes succeeded, CENTCOM will say its blockade is working. The threads-synthesis pass is told to phrase such items as claims rather than facts. Tier 2: X via rss.app.
  • Times of Israel and Jerusalem Post via Bluesky — both English-language Israeli newspapers run active Bluesky accounts that auto-post their headlines with the article URL embedded. We pull them via the AT Protocol's public read API — no key, no free-tier slot, no third-party scraper. Strictly the cleanest mechanism we use anywhere in the codebase. Coverage is English-language Israeli desk reporting on Iran/Lebanon/Gaza, daily and fast. Tier 2: Bluesky AT Protocol.
  • UN OCHA · ReliefWeb — humanitarian situation reports from UN agencies and NGOs (UNHCR, WHO, WFP, ICRC, IOM, OCHA itself), filtered to Iran-tagged English-language reports. The documentary record of the conflict's humanitarian footprint — refugee flows, hospital strikes, shelter capacity, food security. Pulled via the official v2 JSON API with a registered appname. Tier 1: clean API.
  • Iran International — London-based opposition outlet, English-language RSS. Used as a triangulation layer for what's happening inside Iran while direct access to IRNA / Tasnim / IRIB websites is impractical (Iran's domestic internet has been in extended blackout under what the regime calls "Absolute Digital Isolation"). The editorial slant is real and visible; it sits at tier 3 deliberately. Tier 3: RSS, opposition outlet.
  • Iran regime Telegram channels — Khamenei's office (rahbar_enghelab_ir), Iran's state broadcaster IRIB (iribnews), and the IRGC-affiliated Defa Press (defapress_ir). Scraped from the public t.me/s/ web preview, no Telegram account needed. These are regime mouthpieces broadcasting directly — every item is a claim, not a verified fact. Surfaced at tier 4 with a distinct red rule down the left side, smaller headline type, and a per-page "includes claims" badge whenever a thread draws on tier-4 material — so a Khamenei post can never visually blur into a State Department designation. The editor clicks through to Telegram for the original; Persian and Arabic posts are auto-translated by Claude Haiku and the original is one click away. Tier 4: scraped Telegram, regime-claimed.

What's not in (yet, or ever)

  • Hezbollah's Telegram (mmirleb) and other channels with the public web preview disabled. A meaningful slice of ISW's tactical detail comes from Hezbollah's own broadcasts. The channel doesn't expose t.me/s/ publicly, so our scrape can't see it. Reaching it needs the MTProto client API (Telethon), which needs a phone number and Guardian institutional sign-off. Hezbollah specifically is independently gated by UK proscription law. Firmly v2 territory.
  • Iranian state news websites. IRNA, Tasnim, the regime ministries' own sites. Iran's domestic internet remains blackouted; even with proxies the sites are unreliable. We do reach Khamenei's office, IRIB and defapress through their Telegram public previews — see Iran regime Telegram channels above.
  • OSINT and journalist accounts on X. ISW cites named analysts (Tanker Trackers, Manni Fabian, Laura Norman at WSJ, others) and OSINT geolocation accounts. Each would need its own rss.app feed (or paid Twitter API) and we're already using both of our rss.app free-tier slots on IDF and CENTCOM. A real v2 question is whether to upgrade rss.app or write a small Twitter-API adapter against a paid tier.
  • Hebrew Israeli press (Mako, YNet, Channel 12, KAN). ISW pulls from these for the most operational tactical reporting — specific strike locations, IDF-radio scoops, the Hebrew-press 30-minute lead on stories the English wires later pick up. The English Israeli press is now in via Bluesky (Times of Israel, Jerusalem Post above); the Hebrew layer isn't. Polite scrape with translation is realistic and not done here. None of the Hebrew outlets are meaningfully on Bluesky either — the named Hebrew-press reporters who created BS accounts in 2024 have effectively all stopped posting.
  • Wires (Reuters, AFP, BBC Monitoring) and analyst products. Out of scope. The point is the documentary record, not the reporting on it.
  • Geospatial / map content. ISW's reports lean on attack-location maps. We don't have the geolocation pipeline for that and probably won't — it's a different category of work.

How it works

Each source is a small Python module that returns a list of normalised event dicts. A collector runs every adapter, applies a keyword filter to the ones whose feeds aren't already Iran-filtered server-side, de-duplicates by stable id, time-orders, and writes a JSON dump. A renderer turns the JSON into a static HTML page. Both run twice a day on a GitHub Actions cron — 05:30 UTC (the morning briefing, in by 7am UK) and 14:30 UTC (the evening update, in by 4pm UK). The two are stacked on the same dated page rather than overwriting each other: morning is the 24h catch-up read, evening is "what's developed since this morning". Each carries its own day-shape kicker and threads block; one combined events list at the bottom holds the deduped union with shared filters. (GitHub cron is UTC-only, so in UK winter both editions land an hour earlier than the BST timing.)

Two Claude API passes layer on top of the collected events. The first translates Persian, Arabic, and Hebrew Telegram posts into English using claude-haiku-4-5 with a structured-output schema, cached on disk by event id so re-runs don't pay twice for the same post. The second is a single claude-opus-4-7 call per run that clusters the day's events into up to five thematic threads, with a strict editorial-rules system prompt: no interpretation, no "this suggests", no editorial framing, every claim from a regime channel or military-spox source phrased as a claim. The threads sit at the top of the daily brief; the original events sit below. The combined synthesis costs roughly £0.04 a day all-in.

The keyword filter is intentionally cautious. Strong terms ("Iran", "IRGC", "Hezbollah", "Strait of Hormuz", named nuclear sites) trigger inclusion on their own. Weaker proximity terms ("Saudi Arabia", "Jordan", "IAEA", "nuclear deal") are only kept when they appear with a strong term, or alongside another proximity term. False positives are cheap — you skim past them. False negatives are invisible and worse. Sources that are already Iran-filtered server-side (UK Parliament searches by "Iran", Federal Register similarly) skip the local filter; sources that are inherently Iran-focused (Iran International, regime Telegram channels, IDF and CENTCOM during this conflict) skip it too.

The whole thing is open source and small enough to read in one sitting.
